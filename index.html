SECTION 01

A Nation on Prescription

How have prescription rates and therapeutic categories changed over time?

How have medicine categories changed?

Click a medicine group in the legend to highlight its trend. Rates are the mean of 12 monthly rates in each year, not annual totals. Higher lines indicate higher prescription rates, while upward or downward trends show how rates have changed over time. Each coloured line represents a medicine category, allowing readers to compare changes in prescription rates over time.

How to read the scale: The horizontal axis shows the years from 2013 to 2023, while the vertical axis shows the mean monthly prescriptions per resident, starting at 0 and increasing in intervals of 0.05. For example, 0.10 represents approximately 10 prescriptions per 100 residents per month, not 10% of residents.

What happens when a nation's medicine needs begin to shift?

From 2013 to 2023, cardiovascular medicines remained the most frequently prescribed of these six categories, with rates climbing after 2019. Yet antiinfective prescriptions tell a different story, dropping sharply in 2020 as the COVID-19 pandemic began. Together, these contrasting paths reveal that Australia's medicine use is far from a single, uniform trend.

Which Medicine Groups Saw the Biggest Changes Between 2013 and 2023?

Compare average monthly prescriptions per resident across six selected medicine groups. Upward slopes indicate increases, while downward slopes indicate decreases. Each coloured line connects the prescription rates in 2013 and 2023, showing whether a medicine category increased or decreased.

How to read the scale: The vertical axis shows the average number of monthly prescriptions per resident, starting at 0 and increasing in intervals of 0.05. For example, 0.10 represents approximately 10 prescriptions per 100 residents per month, while 0.30 represents 30.

Which medicines changed the most over the decade?

While cardiovascular prescriptions climbed from approximately 0.32 to 0.35 per resident each month, antiinfectives moved in the opposite direction, falling from 0.10 to 0.07. The contrast raises an intriguing question: beyond these long-term changes, how consistently were medicines dispensed from month to month?

How variable are monthly dispensing rates?

Each box summarises the distribution of 132 monthly observations from 2013 - 2023 for a medicine group. This is not a patient-level distribution.

How to read the scale: A value of 0.30 represents approximately 30 prescriptions dispensed per 100 residents each month, not 30% of residents.

Do the most frequently prescribed medicines also fluctuate the most?

Cardiovascular medicines show both the highest monthly dispensing rates and a relatively wide spread. In contrast, respiratory and musculoskeletal medicines remain more tightly clustered, revealing differences in the consistency of medicine use.

The Bigger Picture:

Australia's prescription patterns reveal a changing landscape of medicine use. While some therapeutic categories have grown, others have declined or remained relatively stable. But these national trends only tell part of the story. Do Australians of different ages rely on the same medicines?

SECTION 02

Different Ages, Different Medicines

National totals can conceal substantial differences between age groups.

Which Medicine Groups Are Most Commonly Dispensed Across Age Groups?

Explore differences in average monthly prescription rates across three age groups and six selected medicine categories in 2023. Darker colours represent higher prescription rates per resident.

How to read the scale: A rate of 0.50 means approximately 50 prescriptions dispensed per 100 residents each month, not 50% of residents. Each age group uses a different horizontal scale.

Does age change the medicines Australians rely on?

The answer is striking. Nervous system medicines and antiinfectives lead among children aged 0 - 18, while cardiovascular medicines dominate among those aged 65 and over. What appeared to be a single national prescribing pattern now reveals very different medicine needs across generations.

How Much Do Prescription Rates Differ Between Age Groups?

Each range represents the lowest and highest age-specific prescription rates for a medicine group in 2023. Compare the spread to identify categories with larger differences between age groups.

How to read the scale: A rate of 1.00 represents approximately 100 prescriptions dispensed per 100 residents each month, not 100% of residents. Green dots indicate the lowest rates, purple dots the highest, and connecting lines show the differences between age groups.

Just how wide is the generation gap's medicine use?

Cardiovascular prescriptions show the most dramatic difference, ranging from almost zero to approximately 1.35 prescriptions per resident each month. Meanwhile, antiinfective and respiratory medicines show much narrower gaps. Clearly, age-related differences are far more pronounced for some medicine categories than others.

How Does the Number of Medication Types Vary by Age?

This mosaic plot compares the distribution of PBS medication-type counts across age groups in 2022. Column widths represent estimated age-group population sizes, while segment heights represent percentages within each age group.

How to read the chart: The horizontal axis shows each age group's share of the total population across the selected age groups. Wider columns represent larger populations. The vertical axis shows the percentage of people within each age group reporting different numbers of PBS medication types. Each colour represents a different number of medication types.

Does growing older mean relying on more medicines?

In 2022, around half of children aged 0 - 17 used no PBS medication types, compared with only about 5% of Australians aged 65 and over. Among older Australians, using five or more medication types was far more common. The story shifts from which medicines people use to how many different medicines they rely on.

The Bigger Picture:

Age shapes not only which medicines Australians use, but also how many they rely on. Older Australians generally have higher prescription rates and use more medication types. Yet age is only one piece of the puzzle. Could where Australians live reveal another divide in medicine use?

SECTION 03

The Geography of Medicine Use

Where are prescription rates highest across Australia, and how do they differ between geographic and socioeconomic groups?

How Do Prescription Rates Vary Across Australian States and Territories?

This choropleth map compares the average monthly number of PBS prescriptions per resident across Australian states and territories in 2025. Darker colours indicate higher dispensing rates. Rates are not annual prescription totals.

Does where you live make a difference?

In 2025, Tasmania recorded one of the highest monthly prescription rates, while the Northern Territory stood out with a much lower rate. After seeing how medicine use differs by age, this map reveals another layer of variation in dispensing patterns: Australia's states and territories do not share the same prescription rates.

Where Are Prescription Rates Higher Across Australian States and Territories?

This proportional symbol map compares average monthly PBS prescriptions per resident across Australian states and territories in 2025. Larger circles represent higher prescription rates, not larger populations or greater total prescription counts.

Which parts of Australia stand out?

Tasmania leads with approximately 1.31 monthly prescriptions per resident, more than double the Northern Territory's 0.64. Meanwhile, Queensland and New South Wales share similar rates of 1.04. These differences raise another question: What happens when we reshape the map to make prescription rates, rather than geography, the focus?

What if Australia's geography no longer dominated the picture?

With states representedby circles, Tasmania's high prescription rate becomes impossible to overlook, while the Northern Territory appears much smaller and lighter. This reinforces an important discovery: A state's geographic size does not determine its prescription rate. But could differences within communities reveal an even deeper story?

How Do Prescription Rates Vary by Socioeconomic Quintile?

SEIFA (Socio-Economic Indexes for Areas) is an ABS (Australian Bureau of Statistics) measure of the socioeconomic characteristics of geographic areas. Areas are grouped into five quintiles, from Q1 (most disadvantaged) to Q5 (least disadvantaged). This chart compares average monthly PBS prescriptions per resident across socioeconomic quintiles from 2013 to 2023.

How to read the chart: The horizontal axis shows the years from 2013 to 2023, while the vertical axis groups areas into five socioeconomic quintiles, from Q1 (most disadvantaged) to Q5 (least disadvantaged). Each cell shows the average monthly prescriptions per resident, with darker purple indicating higher rates. For example, 0.618 represents approximately 62 prescriptions per 100 residents per month, not 61.8% of residents.

Does socioeconomic disadvantage reveal another divide?

Across 2013 - 2023, the most disadvantaged areas (Q1) consistently recorded higher prescription rates than the least disadvantaged (Q5). By 2023, the gap was approximately 0.15 monthly prescriptions per resident. This suggests that Australia's medicine use varies not only by location, but also by the socioeconomic characteristics of communities.

The Bigger Picture:

Australia's prescription patterns reveal more than differences between states. From Tasmania's higher dispensing rates to the divide between disadvantaged and advantaged areas, medicine use varies across communities. But these patterns raise a deeper question: Could changes in Australians' chronic health conditions help us understand their evolving healthcare needs?

SECTION 04

Behind the Prescriptions

What do these patterns suggest about changing healthcare needs?

Prescription patterns provide one perspective on healthcare needs. Australia's National Health Survey provides another, showing how the prevalence of selected chronic conditions has changed over time.

How Has the Number of Chronic Conditions Changed Over Time?

Each panel shows the age-standardised percentage of Australians reporting a different number of selected chronic conditions across five ABS survey periods. All panels use the same 0 - 60% vertical scale.

How to read the chart: The horizontal axis shows the ABS survey periods from 2008 to 2022, while the vertical axis shows the age-standardised percentage of Australians reporting selected chronic conditions. Each panel represents a different number of conditions (0, 1, 2 or 3+).

Are more Australians living with chronic conditions?

The proportion reporting no selected chronic conditions fell from approximately 58% in 2008 to 50% in 2022. Meanwhile, the proportions reporting one, two, or three or more conditions gradually increased. This suggests a growing burden of chronic conditions, adding another dimension to the medicine-use patterns explored earlier.

How Has the Prevalence of Chronic Conditions Changed Between 2008 and 2022?

This lollipop chart compares the age-standardised percentage of Australians reporting different numbers of selected chronic conditions in 2008 and 2022. It highlights changes in prevalence across the four condition-count groups. Both panels use the same 0 - 60% scale for direct comparison.

How to read the chart: Each panel represents a survey year (2008 or 2022). The horizontal axis shows the age-standardised percentage of Australians, while the vertical axis groups people by the number of selected chronic conditions reported (0, 1, 2 or 3+). Longer lines and dots positioned further to the right indicate higher percentages.

What changed over these 14 years?

In 2008, nearly 58% of Australians reported no selected chronic conditions. By 2022, that figure had fallen to 50.1%, while the proportion reporting two or more conditions rose from 16.5% to 21.9%. This shift suggests that managing multiple chronic conditions is becoming an increasingly important part of Australia's healthcare story.

What does Australia's changing health landscape look like today?

Imagine a room of 100 Australians. In 2022, approximately 50 would report at least one selected chronic condition. After exploring differences in medicine use across age groups, regions and socioeconomic communities, this finding reminds us why understanding Australia's evolving healthcare needs matters.

One Nation, Different Healthcare Needs

Australia's medicine story is not the same for everyone. Over time, prescription patterns have shifted, revealing differences across medicine categories, age groups, regions and socioeconomic communities . Meanwhile, nearly half of Australians reported at least one selected chronic condition in 2022.

The key takeaway?

Understanding Australia's healthcare needs requires looking beyond national averages. Prescription patterns and chronic health conditions offer different but complementary perspectives on a changing population.
