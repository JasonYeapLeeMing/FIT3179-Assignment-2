SECTION 03

The Geography of Medicine Use

Where are prescription rates highest across Australia, and how do they differ between geographic and socioeconomic groups?

How Do Prescription Rates Vary Across Australian States and Territories?

This choropleth map compares the average monthly number of PBS prescriptions per resident across Australian states and territories in 2025. Darker colours indicate higher dispensing rates. Rates are not annual prescription totals.

Where Are Prescription Rates Higher Across Local Government Areas?

This proportional symbol map shows geographic differences in average monthly PBS prescriptions per resident across Australian Local Government Areas (LGAs) in 2025. Larger circles represent higher prescription rates, not larger populations or greater total prescription counts.

How Do State Prescription Rates Compare Without Geographic Area?

Each circle represents an Australian state or territory. Circle area is proportional to the mean monthly PBS scripts per resident in 2025, while darker blue indicates higher rates. Circles are repositioned to prevent overlap while approximately preserving geographic relationships. Distances and positions are schematic.

How Do Prescription Rates Vary by Socioeconomic Quintile?

SEIFA (Socio-Economic Indexes for Areas) is an ABS measure of the socioeconomic characteristics of geographic areas. Areas are grouped into five quintiles, from Q1 (most disadvantaged) to Q5 (least disadvantaged).

This chart compares average monthly PBS prescriptions per resident across socioeconomic quintiles from 2013 to 2023. SEIFA describes areas, not the socioeconomic circumstances of individual residents.

Sources: AIHW HWE-098, PBS prescriptions by geographic area; AIHW HWE-099, PBS Rates by Remoteness and SEIFA; Australian Bureau of Statistics, ASGS 2021 State and Territory boundaries and ASGS 2025 Local Government Area boundaries. Geographic dispensing rates should not be interpreted as direct measures of healthcare access or disease prevalence.

SECTION 04

Behind the Prescriptions

What do these patterns suggest about changing healthcare needs?

Prescription patterns provide one perspective on healthcare needs. Australia's National Health Survey provides another, showing how the prevalence of selected chronic conditions has changed over time.

How Has the Number of Chronic Conditions Changed Over Time?

Each panel shows the age-standardised percentage of Australians reporting a different number of selected chronic conditions across five ABS survey periods. All panels use the same 0 - 60% vertical scale.

How Many Australians Report at Least One Selected Chronic Condition?

Each square represents approximately 1% of Australians in 2022. The 49.9% survey estimate is rounded to 50 highlighted squares.

What Does This Tell Us?

The proportion of Australians reporting at least one selected chronic condition increased from 42.2% in 2007 - 08 to 49.9% in 2022. This suggests that managing long-term health conditions remains an important consideration for Australia's healthcare system. However, these survey results cannot establish why prescription dispensing rates have changed.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, National Health Survey 2022, Table 1.3. Age-standardised proportions of persons.
